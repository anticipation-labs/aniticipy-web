Anticipy revision 5 — research notes

Checked 8 September 2026. The aim is immediate category recognition and a purchase decision that can be understood within roughly 30 seconds. Those are design and testing targets, not scientifically established time thresholds. Sources below are original studies, researchers’ own publications, W3C guidance, and Baymard’s direct usability research. No source demonstrates conversion lift for Anticipy.

**Five implementation recommendations**

1. **Make the answer visible from the first frame.** If using “Not a note taker. An action taker.”, make ACTION TAKER the dominant immediate phrase, accompanied by “AI pendant” and one concrete capability. Do not initially show only NOTE TAKER while the actual category waits for animation. Tuch et al. experimentally found that visual complexity and familiar structure affect aesthetic judgments within milliseconds. **Limit:** the dependent variable was aesthetic appraisal, not comprehension, trust or purchase readiness. The 0.4-second target should concern hierarchy and recognition; understanding requires a separate test. [Original research](https://research.google/pubs/the-role-of-visual-complexity-and-prototypicality-regarding-first-impression-of-websites-working-towards-understanding-aesthetic-judgments/)

2. **Explain three different situations, not three versions of “follow-through.”** Use short, concrete examples from work, family scheduling and personal administration, restricted to supported capabilities. Each needs a recognizable spoken need and a distinct result. Packard and Berger’s experiments found that relevant, concrete service language improved customer responses; specificity ceased helping when the response failed to address the customer’s actual need. **Limit:** these were service interactions, not landing-page tests. Applying the principle to copy is an inference; “more detail” is not automatically better. [Original study](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/47/5/787/5873524)

3. **Replace fabricated verification with inspectable evidence.** Remove invented “Sent & verified” receipts. Use actual product demonstrations where available; identify concept demonstrations honestly. Keep the same pendant shape, scale and finish across close-up, worn view and demonstration. Baymard’s testing found that contextual imagery helps buyers judge size, while genuine imagery of the specific product in use supports evaluation. **Limit:** these findings do not turn generated imagery into evidence of functionality or customer endorsement. The protected lifestyle photograph supplies visual context; its presence alone proves no software capability. [Product-scale research](https://baymard.com/blog/in-scale-product-images), [Real-use imagery research](https://baymard.com/blog/integrate-social-media-visuals-on-product-page)

4. **Use motion to explain a change; keep the message accessible throughout.** Animate a relationship between an utterance and its corresponding result, or a physical property of the pendant. Keep headline, capability and CTA readable without completing a scroll sequence. Give demonstrations a readable resting state and direct controls. Heer and Robertson’s controlled experiments found that animated transitions could improve graphical perception. **Limit:** those tasks involved data graphics, not product marketing; they do not justify decorative motion or predict sales. Respect reduced motion. W3C’s interaction-animation criterion is AAA, and distinguishes necessary scrolling from additional nonessential animation. [Original experiments](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/animated-transitions-in-statistical-data-graphics/), [W3C guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)

5. **Put the purchase explanation beside a predictable CTA.** Show payment today, estimated delivery, included hardware/service, subsequent service cost, refund timing, and required phone/app dependencies in one compact, reachable summary. Baymard’s current research identifies unexpected costs, unclear totals, trust and checkout complexity as abandonment reasons. **Limit:** these are broad ecommerce findings, including self-reported abandonment reasons; they establish neither a 30-second purchase deadline nor an Anticipy-specific effect size. [Current primary research](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)

The headline contrast should describe Anticipy’s purpose rather than claim competitors cannot act. Pocket currently markets automatic updates to calendars, tasks, documents and AI context; Plaud One markets actions through connected tools. [Pocket](https://heypocket.com/pages/pocket), [Plaud One](https://www.plaud.ai/products/plaud-one)

**Saturation, lightness and arousal**

Wilms and Oberfeld (2018; published online 2017) independently varied hue, saturation and brightness. Sixty-two participants viewed each stimulus for 30 seconds. Higher saturation and brightness were associated with higher reported arousal, with interactions between dimensions; skin-conductance responses also differed. **Limit:** this was an LED color-field experiment using red, green, blue and achromatic stimuli, not cream websites or purchase behavior. It does not identify an optimal CSS color. [Author-hosted original paper](https://www.staff.uni-mainz.de/oberfeld/downloads/Wilms-Oberfeld2018_Article_ColorAndEmotionEffectsOfHueSat.pdf)

Valdez and Mehrabian (1994), using Munsell color chips, also found strong saturation and brightness effects. Higher saturation was associated with greater arousal, but their brightness–arousal relationship was negative, unlike the later display experiment. **Implication:** the differing stimulus contexts and findings argue against a universal “brighter is calmer” rule. Arousal also should not be equated with anxiety, preference or purchasing. [Original study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7996122/)

For this revision, reduce the yellow chroma of large UI surfaces while preserving the supplied warm lifestyle photograph. Use near-neutral ivory or white for breathing room, charcoal for readable demonstration surfaces and text, and warmth through restrained linen/sand details and the photograph. This is a response to the user’s art direction and a testable design judgment—not a scientific claim that beige creates trust. Treat CSS lightness, display luminance, perceived brightness and saturation as related but distinct concepts; do not transfer laboratory coefficients into design tokens.

Maintain WCAG AA contrast: 4.5:1 for ordinary text, 3:1 for qualifying large text, and 3:1 for meaningful control/state graphics against adjacent colors. Evaluate actual composed backgrounds, including transparency and photograph crops. Status words/icons should carry meaning without requiring blue or green. [Text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), [Non-text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html), [Use of color](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)

**Quick validation**

After brief first-screen exposure, ask target buyers to describe the object and its purpose without prompts. Then allow 30 seconds and ask what they could use it for, which capabilities are available, what requires approval, what they pay and when delivery is expected. Count correct answers and false assumptions. Test tone, demonstration and palette changes separately where practical; a preference poll or a higher click rate alone does not establish comprehension or commercial success.
